How to collect answers

Collect answers as the paper did: one answer per question with the prompt as given; greedy decoding with up to 3,072 new tokens, or, for reasoning models, temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95 and top-k 20 with up to 12,288 tokens. The author responses found that temperature does not move a model's position but a system prompt does, so reference and query answers must share a system prompt.

Question ids, benchmarks and pool membership: questions.csv. Prompts, gold answers and graders are in the code release (data/core/probe.jsonl). The encoder reads the 900 pool questions and ignores the rest; at least 50 must be answered.

How it works

BULLPEN embeds a model from three channels of its answers to a fixed probe: its correctness bits, the text of the questions it got right (a correctness-weighted mean question embedding), and the text of its own answers (mean BGE answer embedding). Closed-form multi-block PLS-SVD fits per-channel loadings, and the code concatenates each channel's z-scored scores: 12 + 10 + 10 dimensions for BULLPEN-fused, 16 + 16 for BULLPEN-bits+q (bits and question text only).

Everything shown is fitted with the released code on fold 0, seed 0 of the paper's strict protocol (publisher-grouped folds over models; the 900 pool questions): 208 training models and 52 held out. A new model is placed by the paper's fold-in: a ridge solve of its observed bits on the frozen bit loadings, its question block from those bits, and a projection of its mean answer embedding. Every map is linear, so the page evaluates them directly from exported weights; no model runs here. The heads are the paper's battery readouts (standardised ridge, or logistic for base vs chat) refitted once on the training models.

The middle view is a PCA of the 260 placements in the selected space, a linear map, so an uploaded model is projected exactly. Nearest neighbours are computed by cosine in the full 32-d space, not in the view.

Limits. The submission scores the heads on held-out models from the 260 open-weight models; the author responses add nine closed models placed without refitting (see Reported results). One split and one seed are shown, so per-model values here are single draws, not the paper's averages over 15 runs. The base-vs-chat label comes from the model name, as in the paper.
